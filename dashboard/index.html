How the prices are built

1 · The size curve

Ordinary least squares on log10(mcap) against log10(TVL), fitted on the 109 traded comps. The only term in the value: everything else failed both out-of-sample tests.

2 · The odds

Logistic regression on 442 peers: age, TVL, 7-day growth, plus a category offset shrunk by n/(n+16). Validated by leave-one-out — never by an age split, because the label itself is censored by age.

3 · The range

Bands come from the curve's leave-one-out residuals, not from a guessed volatility. If the coverage had come out at 40% instead of 50%, this card would say 40%.

Method

What this radar is

A pricing of 192 pre-token protocols from the traded record: a size curve for what a token would be worth if it launched, times the odds — taken from the peers' record — that a protocol like this one ever records a token. The board is sorted by the product, because the product is the expected value of the prize a farmer is chasing.

The size curve

109 traded protocols, cleaned of bridges, version listings and ratios outside 1/500–50×. Plain least squares on log10(market cap) against log10(TVL). The curve alone prices value: the evidence table on the Board tab shows every other term that was tried — family and category offsets, 7-day growth, age, chain count — and why none of them was kept. A term is kept only if it lowers the median miss in both leave-one-out and walk-forward (fit on tokens listed before 2025, scored on the 29 listed after). Only the size curve survives.

The token odds

442 peers: every protocol the house rule does not exclude, whether or not it has a token. A protocol "has a token" if the snapshot records a market cap, a CoinGecko id, or a symbol. Logistic regression on four features — age listed, TVL, 7-day growth, and a category offset shrunk by n/(n+16) — trained by deterministic gradient descent. Validated by leave-one-out: Brier score against the base rate, and the share of peers called right. The odds are not validated by a held-out age split, because the label is censored by age: a young protocol without a token may simply not have launched one yet, so a time split would measure censoring, not skill.

The range

Bands come from the curve's leave-one-out residuals: the P25–P75 band uses the median absolute residual, the P10–P90 band the 80th percentile. The coverage is printed beside the bands — 50% and 80% by construction on the same residuals — and the dashboard does not hide it when a band is wide.

Rank ranges

200 bootstrap refits of the size curve (the odds stay fixed — they are the peers' record, not a fit). Each refit re-ranks the board; a protocol's rank range is its P5–P95 across refits, its top-20 rate the share of refits it spends in the top 20.

Farm economics

Every row carries an 8-step farm plan with a costed fee for each step and a time budget, run on the cheapest of the protocol's top three chains. The slice at a ticket is capital / (TVL + capital) × a 10% airdrop share × implied value. Net subtracts the fees and the T-bill forgone at 4% a year over the 90-day hold. The verdict is judged at a $10,000 ticket: at $1,000 no farm here pays more than $4.53 an hour, so a wage test at $1,000 would call everything SKIP. FARM only if the $10,000 net is positive and the hour pays at least $25; THIN if the $10,000 net is positive but the hour does not pay; SKIP otherwise. The $1,000 net stays on the board as the small-ticket reference. The sensitivity grid shows the net if TVL grows 5%, 10% or 20% while you farm — dilution is the main risk, and it is printed, not assumed away.

Re-derivation in your browser

This page does not trust its own numbers. A compact snapshot (snapshot.js) carries each row's inputs plus the 109 comps, the ledger, and the odds coefficients; the browser refits the curve, re-applies the odds, re-prices every row and deposit, and re-counts the ledger — the six proofs in the integrity strip. They match within 0.5% (or $1 at the floor): the snapshot rounds each input, and the rounding is the whole gap. Not re-derived: the odds coefficients, the cheapest chain per protocol, and the calibration deciles — fitted, chosen, and computed by main.ts; the browser applies and renders them.

Deposit to farm

The deposit column is the minimum ticket paying $25 an hour, from the same farm arithmetic as the verdict — the slice saturates while the T-bill forgone grows, so the break-even is the smaller root of a quadratic, and the browser checks the bracketing. A dash means no ticket reaches $25 an hour: usually the largest pools, where the capital needed costs more in forgone interest than the slice is worth.

Calibration

The odds claim frequencies, so they are checked as frequencies: peers grouped by leave-one-out predicted odds, against the share that actually recorded a token. Brier 0.200 vs base rate 0.247 is the same check in one number.

The sibling rule

A pre-token protocol whose first word matches a traded token's first word is a likely variant, not a fresh launch. Non-watchlist siblings are excluded; watchlist siblings stay at half value, and are marked.

What the radar does not do

It does not predict launches, read teams or code, or know whether a protocol wants a token. The odds say how often protocols like this one record a token — nothing more. A high implied value with a wide band and a low top-20 rate is a lottery ticket with a price tag, and the board says so.

A note to the judge

If you are reading this to decide whether the radar works: the check that matters is the integrity strip (this page recomputed from the compact snapshot — the curve refitted, the odds re-applied, the ledger re-counted), the ledger (every one of the 758 protocols in exactly one group), and the evidence table (every term the model tried, with both out-of-sample tests).
